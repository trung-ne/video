# Style guide for `refer/frames`

Use this guide after visually sampling the current project frames. The frames remain the authority; this file describes the stable, high-level grammar to transfer without copying particular designs.

## Transferable traits

- Bold, dark, slightly uneven hand-drawn contours.
- Flat color regions with little or no modeled lighting.
- Simple geometric bodies and props that read instantly.
- Sparse facial features; expression relies on brows, eye direction, mouth, pose, and one small reaction cue.
- Dry, understated comedy and rapid editorial storytelling.
- Limited palette and low prop density around one focal action.
- Intentional asymmetry and naive-looking shapes controlled enough to stay legible.

## Originality boundary

Do not reproduce a recognizable character, unique facial layout, exact silhouette, signature palette pairing, prop arrangement, pose, or frame composition. Do not trace or perform a cosmetic recolor. Build a fresh design system from the user's description and document at least four visible differences from the nearest reference.

For characters, vary all of these:

- Overall silhouette and body proportions.
- Head, muzzle, eyes, ears, limbs, and tail construction.
- Dominant and secondary colors.
- Markings, accessory, or signature feature.

For props and scenes, vary all of these:

- Shape language and proportions.
- Color hierarchy.
- Camera angle or spatial arrangement.
- Focal detail and surrounding support elements.

## Quality bar

- Readable at 128 px for isolated assets and thumbnails.
- One dominant visual idea per image.
- Strong separation between subject and background.
- Clean anatomy and purposeful overlaps.
- No gradients, glossy rendering, dense texture, stray text, logos, signatures, or watermarks unless explicitly requested.
